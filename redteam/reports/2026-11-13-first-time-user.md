# First-time-user red-team report: the Studio, 2026-11-13

Reviewer: the confused first-time user (one of the three red-team reviewers in CLAUDE.md rule 12). I played a person with no design vocabulary and no coding knowledge. I received the built page and the code, not the builder's conclusions. My job was to find problems, so this report is mostly problems.

## Result in plain words

In a desktop window (1440×900) with a mouse, a newcomer can choose a direction, walk the 22 Steps, reach the Summary, and save a file. Two things stop that from being a success:

1. **In any window up to 1,099 pixels wide the Step screens and the Summary cannot be used.** That includes 200% zoom (720×450) and a phone (400×860). The page grows and shrinks without end, and the Options, the "Pick" button and "Save the project file" move thousands of screens away.
2. **The main flow quietly replaces the direction the person chose.** On 2 to 8 Steps of every direction, the Step opens on a standard Option that is not the person's design, and both ways forward ("Pick … and continue" and "Skip: take the default") apply it. A dark direction turns light with one press.

With a keyboard alone the whole journey is possible. It cost me 225 key presses, the focus mark vanished at eight moments, and the on-screen shortcut "Enter picks" does not work in the state the Studio leaves you in after every pick.

| Severity | Count | Meaning in this report |
|---|---|---|
| blocker | 2 | A newcomer cannot complete a core task. |
| major | 33 | Likely confusion or a wrong action. |
| minor | 15 | Polish. |

The five most damaging findings: **FTU-01** (narrow windows), **FTU-02** (the flow replaces the direction), **FTU-03 with FTU-04** ("Skip" changes the design, and "default" means five things), **FTU-05 with FTU-06** (slider adjustments are lost or mislabelled), **FTU-09** ("Enter picks" does nothing).

### Words used in this report

- **Direction:** one of the starting looks on the first screen ("Graphite Precision", "Soft Daylight", …).
- **Step:** one screen that asks for one design decision. **Option:** one of the choices on a Step. **Card:** the box that shows one Option (small picture, number, name).
- **Large preview:** the big picture of the sample website in the middle of a Step screen. **Small picture:** the picture on a card.
- **On show:** the Option that the large preview displays at this moment (the screen says "Showing: …").
- **Focus:** the control that the keyboard will act on. The Studio marks it with a blue ring. **Tab stop:** one control that the Tab key lands on.
- **List of Steps:** the column on the left of a Step screen.

### Step 7, Typeface: "What voice do the letters have?" (FTU-37, minor)

Verdict: a newcomer can choose between letters with small feet and letters without, because the text explains both and the pictures show it. A newcomer cannot choose among the five Options without feet.

- "a serif (letters with small feet) feels established; a geometric sans (round, even letters) feels friendly; a grotesque feels neutral." Two terms are explained in brackets; "a grotesque" is not.
- "Pairing one face for headings with a quieter one for reading text is the classic move." "face" is a new word for typeface, and "the classic move" assumes the reader knows the trade.
- Option "Warm editorial": "Fraunces headings (a soft, characterful serif) over Public Sans text." Two font names, and "over" used in a trade sense.
- Option "Formal": "Playfair Display headings (high-contrast, elegant) over DM Sans text." "high-contrast" means thick and thin strokes here; in the top bar "contrast" means text against its background.
- Option "Technical": "Space Grotesk is a frequent "alternative" AI pick; it is distinctive but no longer rare." This assumes the reader knows what AI tools pick.
- Option "Civic": "Watch for: Unremarkable by design." Is that a risk or a merit?

## What worked well

- The eye exam. "which is better, 1 or 2?" explains the method in one line, and number keys flip the large preview at once on directions without entrance motion.
- The direction cards: large pictures that differ clearly, and "Best for / Watch for" in mostly plain words.
- Each Step asks one question in plain words: "How do visitors move around the site?", "What should the eye find first?"
- Every Option says "Choose it when" and "Watch for". The advice is honest about risks.
- "Showing: …" and "Pick “…” and continue" always name the Option.
- Every control of the Studio shows a clear blue focus ring. The new "Skip the preview and go to the Options" button removes 17 Tab stops.
- Work is kept without asking. A refresh returned me to the same Step with my Decisions; reopening the page landed on the first open Step.
- A wrong file is refused with a clear sentence, and the open project stays untouched.
- "Start a new project…" asks before it clears.
- Typing a digit in the project name does not flip the Options.
- At 720 px and 400 px nothing scrolls sideways and no text is cut.
- When copying is blocked, the Studio says so and shows the text to copy by hand.
- The note about clashing picks says what to do.
