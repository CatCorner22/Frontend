### Notes on individual rows

- **Q4 and Q5 are the rows where Google's structured-data pages say "must" (local-business: "You must include the required properties"; intro-structured-data: "You must include all the required properties").** Even there the consequence is eligibility for a display feature, not ranking. ## Common SEO advice found without a source in these pages

The Floor should not write any of these as a rule. I list them because a builder will meet them.

- **A meta description of 150 to 160 characters.** Google says "There's no limit" and that snippets are cut to fit the device width. MDN gives no description length. The only figure for title or description length in the pages read is MDN's "about the first 55–60 characters" for titles; I saw no source cited next to it. (Other length figures exist for other fields, for example priceRange under 100 characters.)
- **A `keywords` meta tag.** Google: "Google Search doesn't use the keywords meta tag." Do not require it.
- **A minimum word count, or a keyword density target.** Google: "there's no magical word count target"; the helpful-content page asks whether you write to a word count and answers "No, we don't".
- **Keywords in the domain name or URL path.** Google: they have "hardly any effect beyond appearing in breadcrumbs".
- **Exactly one `<h1>` as a ranking rule, or headings in strict order as a ranking rule.** Google: "it doesn't matter if you're using them out of order" for Search. MDN supports one `<h1>` for structure and accessibility, not ranking.
- **A longer title "performs better".** MDN says "a longer, descriptive title performs better than short or generic titles" without a source. Google says only that titles should be descriptive and concise.
- **Alt text capped at 125 characters, or title identical to `<h1>`.** Not in the pages read.
- **A sitemap and a robots.txt file are needed.** Google: a sitemap "isn't required"; robots.txt is something you create "if you decided that you need one".
- **Citation and "name, address, phone consistency" across other directories, and a Google Business Profile.** These matter to local search in common advice, but none of the pages I read covers them. They are outside the page itself and outside this Floor.
- **E-E-A-T as a ranking factor.** The starter guide answers "Thinking E-E-A-T is a ranking factor" with "No, it's not." The helpful-content page adds that trust is the most important aspect and that Google's systems give more weight to strong E-E-A-T for topics that affect health, money, or safety. Treat these as reasons for a person to check content accuracy, not as a program check.

## What a program cannot check

- Whether the page will appear in search results, at what position, or with what title or snippet. Google writes the title link and the snippet itself and may replace the owner's text (title-link page, "Common issues"; snippet page, "How snippets are created").
- Whether Google has found, crawled, or indexed the page. That needs Search Console data or a `site:` search, run by the owner.
- Whether structured data is eligible for a rich result. The Rich Results Test is a Google web tool; the Fitter may run only its own JSON checks (Q4).
- Whether the text is true, original, helpful, and shows real experience (Q15). A model may judge tone and clarity; the Honest receipt must label that "judged by the model". Accuracy of health claims, prices, and hours needs a person.
- Whether alt text matches the picture, and whether link text and headings help a reader.
- Whether the owner owns the right to the images and text.
- Site-level work: a sitemap, redirects from other addresses, a Google Business Profile, links from other sites, page speed in the field. Speed belongs to the performance Floor.

## Conflicts or doubts

1. **Few musts, many recommendations.** The Google pages describe nearly everything here as a best practice. If the Floor says "requirement", the owner should know that for search most items are recommendations. I suggest the Floor wording "checked: present" and "not checked: whether it helps".
2. **Heading rules.** Google says heading order does not matter for Search and there is "no magical, ideal amount of headings"; MDN and WCAG ask for one `<h1>` and no skipped levels. Q7 rests on accessibility and on Google's example of making the main title clear, not on a ranking effect.
3. **Title length.** MDN says about 55 to 60 characters show; Google says there is no limit and the cut-off depends on device width. Q3 has no length test for that reason. If the owner wants one, it is a convention, not a source rule.
4. **Canonical.** Google says no canonical method is required and it picks one when none is given, but it recommends a self-referential canonical. The exporter cannot know the production address. Decide whether the Floor leaves the check as "not checked until the address is known" or asks the owner for the address in the Brief.
5. **Structured data sub-type.** The Google page says to use the most specific LocalBusiness sub-type and lists examples (Restaurant, DaySpa, HealthClub). It does not list the sub-types. The valid list is on schema.org, which was not an allowed host. The builder must confirm `Dentist` (or the owner's sub-type) there before the check can fail on an unknown type.
6. **Review markup on a business's own page.** The LocalBusiness page limits `review` and `aggregateRating` to sites that capture reviews about other businesses. Writing review markup for the owner's own business would likely break Google's guidance; Q4 says do not add it.
7. **Documentation dates.** Dates shown are close to the record date (Google pages from 2025-12 to 2026-10). Google changes these pages often; the owner's Catalog needs a review date per record, as the project rules already say.
8. **Search engines other than Google.** Every Google page here describes Google only. The robots meta page says other engines may treat rules differently. The starter guide hopes the practices help "search engines (not just Google)" but gives no test.
9. **Statements of behaviour graded "standard text".** Q2 and Q10 are Google's descriptions of what its crawler does, not a standard body's requirement. If the owner prefers to reserve "standard text" for WCAG and the HTML specification, grade those two "convention".
10. **Reading method.** I read converted text, not the rendered pages. Tabs, collapsed sections, or diagrams on a page could hold text that the converted version shows in a different order. I saw no sign of missing sections, but I cannot rule it out.
11. **YMYL.** A dental practice gives health information. The helpful-content page says Google weighs trust more for such topics. The Floor should not claim any health content is acceptable; a qualified person must check it.
