## What a program cannot check

- Whether alt text, link text, labels, headings, and the page title are meaningful, true, and helpful. A program can only see that text exists.
- Whether text over photos, video, gradients, or animated backgrounds stays readable at every point.
- Whether the focus indicator is easy to see in context, and whether keyboard order feels logical.
- Whether custom widgets (menus, carousels, accordions, dialogs) work by keyboard and announce correctly in a screen reader. Testing with a screen reader and with voice control is a person's job.
- Whether color carries meaning in charts, maps, or images.
- Media: captions, transcripts, audio description, and flashing content (1.2.x, 2.3.1). The Floor in this record does not cover them; a business home page with video needs a person to check them. The W3C ACT list shows only proposed rules for most of these.
- Time limits, auto-moving carousels and tickers (2.2.2), content that appears on hover or focus (1.4.13), error messages after a failed form (3.3.1, 3.3.3), and consistent navigation across pages (3.2.3). None is in the Floor above. A person must check them.
- Plain-language reading level and cognitive load.
- Anything that appears only after sign-in or after several steps, unless a script walks those steps.

web.dev says it in one line: "Automated tools don't catch all of the accessibility errors in your product" (https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/test-automated). That is a statement from a teaching course, not a measured percentage.

## Conflicts or doubts

1. **Large-text size.** WCAG Understanding 1.4.3 says 18 pt or 14 pt bold is about 24 px and 18.5 px. MDN's button page says 18.66 px and bold, or 24 px. web.dev says 18.5 px bold. 14 pt is 18.67 px exactly, so 18.5 px is a rounding. Recommendation: the exporter should state 24 px regular or 18.66 px bold as the large-text line, which is safe against all three.
2. **No rounding.** The 1.4.3 and 1.4.11 Understanding pages say computed ratios must not be rounded (4.499 fails 4.5). Many tools round to two digits. The Fitter should compare the unrounded value.
3. **Where the background color comes from.** WCAG note 3 and 4 under "contrast ratio" say that if only one of text color or background color is set, the check fails. A coding tool must always set both together. A page with a background image has no single background color; the program can only estimate it.
4. **axe-core scope.** The ACT implementations page lists axe-core 4.14.0 under "WCAG 2.1". WCAG 2.2 adds 2.4.11 and 2.5.8 (the target-size rule exists in axe to my knowledge, but I could not confirm it from an allowed host). The web.dev testing module also says it uses WCAG 2.1 and has not been updated. The builder must run `axe.getRules()` and keep only rules that exist, and must write the Fit check for A9, A17, and A18 as its own computed measures if axe lacks them.
5. **Criteria with no W3C test rule.** The ACT page says "No ACT Rules available for this criterion yet" for 1.4.11, 2.4.3, 2.4.11, 2.5.8, and 3.3.2 (and for 1.4.1). Checks A3 (the visible-label part), A6, A9, A16, A17, A18 are therefore tool-specific methods, not W3C-endorsed ones. They are still derived from the success-criterion text.
6. **Focus indicator.** 2.4.7 asks only that the indicator be visible. The 3:1 requirement comes from 1.4.11 as the Understanding page explains it (MDN's `:focus-visible` page also says this, but cites WCAG 2.1). The stricter 2.4.13 Focus Appearance is AAA and is not in the Floor. The 1.4.11 page also notes that a color-only change inside a button is not tested by 1.4.11.
7. **Zoom.** WCAG asks for 200%. MDN says the best practice is to allow 5x. The Floor should follow WCAG (200%, no `user-scalable=no`) and may add 500% as an advisory line.
8. **Skip link.** 2.4.1 is written about "repeated on multiple web pages", so a single home page is arguably outside it. The criterion also accepts landmarks or headings instead of a skip link, and the Understanding page says a skip link may be unnecessary when navigation sits at the bottom. A11 asks for a skip link plus `main` because the home page is the first page of a set. That choice is a convention, not text of the standard. MDN's "Operable" page, in the 2.4.1 part, still contains the placeholder line "Need to add a section on skip links" (unfinished text).
9. **Structure rules (A10).** The criterion 1.3.1 is normative, but "one `h1`" and "no skipped heading levels" are axe best-practice rules, not requirements of WCAG 2.2. That is why A10 carries the grade "convention".
10. **Label rule overlap.** 3.3.2 wants visible labels or instructions; 4.1.2 wants a name that may be hidden; 2.5.3 wants the visible label inside the name. Per the Understanding 3.3.2 page, `aria-label` alone can pass 4.1.2 and fail 3.3.2. The Floor should therefore require a visible `label`, not just an `aria-label`. 2.5.3 is not in the Floor; its only W3C rule is proposed.
11. **ACT status.** The W3C ACT page says ACT rules are "informative", not required to determine conformance. Using them as the measuring method is a project choice.
12. **Frequency order.** The order of A1 to A20 rests on my background knowledge, not on a page I read (see the note under the heading "Candidate Floor requirements").
13. **WCAG 2.2 removed 4.1.1 Parsing.** Older tools and the web.dev course may still report parsing errors. Do not write a parsing check into the Floor.
