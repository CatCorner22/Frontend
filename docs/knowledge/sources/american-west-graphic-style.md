## Summary

What the search engine's summary says about the "Old West" look in print and on screens. It matters to this project because the Direction "Frontier Gazette" rests on it. The evidence is weak: only the search summary was seen, and most of the pages sell fonts.

## Key concepts

- The lettering of the look comes from wood type: display faces that began in England in the 1820s and 1830s. Films and television later made them the public image of the Old West.
- The summary names three families of lettering: slab serifs (heavy, blocky feet), Tuscan and other ornamental faces (split, forked feet), and "fat faces" (letters with very thick and very thin strokes).
- Posters of the period were dense: several sizes and styles stacked in one centered layout, often with wide letter spacing.
- The palette is earthy and sun-faded: browns, tans, sepia, rust, cream, black, and faded blue. One page adds cool Southwest colors against a warm base.
- Textures suggested: wood grain, leather, burlap, and a slight worn, hand-printed look.
